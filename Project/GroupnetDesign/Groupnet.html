GroupNet

Redesign of an Insurance Registration

My Role

Role

UX Designer

Stakeholders

Business Analyst

Developer

SEO Team

As part of Great-West Life’s goal to help alleviate an increase in call center volume regarding GroupNet registration, a new registration process for GroupNet is designed and prototyped.

The redesigned desktop registration process of GroupNet will help address current and potential issues involving various stakeholders. The end result is a decrease in call volume regarding GroupNet registration, an intuitive user interface and an improved user experience.

As this issue needed to be resolved urgently, this project was a joint effort with senior UX members from the London office. My main contribution was designing improvements for the registration flow that had a high impact, were cost-effective and could be deployed in a matter of weeks by the development team.

High fidelity prototype of GroupNet Log in Screen

Problem Definition

GroupNet, a Great-West Life insurance application is a system widely used across Canada specifically for group benefits members in small and large businesses. GroupNet is used as an online group insurance solution to help users submit claims, check claim status and other insurance related tasks. GroupNet is primarily accessed through a desktop environment but with a recent update to the IOS app store, an increasing number of users are using GroupNet through their mobile device

Recently, product owners of GroupNet have been notified of an increase in call volume with regards to registration. This is most likely due to an on-going migration of users from the old application to a new and improved GroupNet experience which requires them to re-register. The surge in calls has led to many unsatisfied users who are unable to receive help or submit their claims. Due to the poor user experience, GroupNet’s reputation has been damaged.

“Old GroupNet” Log in and Sign up Page

Product Goal

To satisfy user pain points with the current system, design changes must help clarify public confusion on which GroupNet to register with, successfully allow legacy users to register on the new GroupNet platform, link their existing GroupNet accounts and reduce user error during the registration process.

User Experience Goals

Using the Microsoft’s Desirability toolkit, I was able to make a list of user experience goals that I wanted users to feel when using the new system. This toolkit assisted in identifying what emotions we want to elicit from the user before, during and after the use of the system

  • Users should feel confident on how to register with all the required information needed beforehand from their employer
  • Users should feel that the actions of registration, logging in and password recovery are easy, intuitive and self-serving actions
  • Users should feel knowledgeable of what is happening to the “GroupNet” website and avoid registering for an obsolete system

Process

1. Current Registration Flow

To get a high-level view of the entire user flow through the GroupNet registration process, I proceeded to map out different scenarios and possible flows

GroupNet sign in and forgot password flow diagram

2. Understanding our Users

To get a better understanding on the problem and what the root issue most users are struggling with, I turned to call center employees at Canada Life for answers. Over a 4-day observation period, we had call center employees record log their conversations and what users were struggling with. To get a full breakdown on the situation, issues were sorted and categorized appropriately

  • Plan member needed a plan ID and member ID to complete verification – 33.3%
  • Plan member used new information that has not yet been updated in our system – 25.9%
  • Plan member used the wrong postal code or an old address – 18.5%
  • Plan member used a middle name not on file – 11.2%
  • Plan member not internally recognized or authenticated by company – 11.1%

3. Understanding Google Analytics

I turned to the digital analytics team to extract overall user trends and to no surprise view, a recent decline in successful user account linking and successful registrations

GroupNet user trends from Google Analytics

The digital team also helped me pinpoint and extract user pain points which coincide with the Canada Life call center account linking data

GroupNet account linking errors from Google Analytics

4. Whiteboard User Flow Sketching Session

With key features and user pain points identified on the user flow, I did a whiteboard session with my colleagues to decide what changes would make sense from a development, product manager, business and design perspective

Live and interactive user flow whiteboard session

5. New GroupNet Registration Flow

After consolidation with different stakeholders from the development, business, product and design teams, a new registration, homepage and log in flows were mapped

New and proposed GroupNet Registration Flow

6. Final Design Feature Explanation

With the introduction of the GroupNet logo, primary action and an explanation of what’s going on not only informs the user of why they need to register again but alerts them of changes going on within the company. This prevents users from registering with the old service and invites them to register or re-register with the new GroupNet

Redesigned old GroupNet homepage

The new homepage has been fitted with a dropdown of the most asked questions that helps address the call center questions Canada Life has been receiving. The homepage also features a primary action registration button to encourage primary action for users coming from the Old GroupNet experience looking to register

New GroupNet registration and help section

Users are also shown a plan member and member ID card before as well as during the registration process allowing them to have all the materials necessary to successfully create and link their account. The registration process is also broken down into multiple steps to reduce the cognitive load for users.

Plan member card and registration breakdown

8. Conclusion

Upon conducting website maintenance, within a one-week period overall call volume related issue involving GroupNet were reduced by 32%. Within a 1-month period over 67% of unique link clicks represented the “start registration” primary action button with a 59% decrease in traffic towards the Old GroupNet homepage.

Changes resulted in an overall 20% increase in successful account linking and 70% less traffic towards the GroupNet help page hosted on the Great-West Life site with the integrated homepage help center. It is evident that subtle design changes in the user flow resulted in a more autonomous registration, log in, lost password and edge case handling within GroupNet.
